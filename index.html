Council: can many cheap models match one strong one?

I wanted to know if a group of very cheap models could do what one strong model does, and where they would fall short, so I built a test bench to find out.

What I measured
I ran 220 samples across 18 problems, and then head-to-head trials on an open research problem.
What I caught
When I checked the grader I found four bugs that were making the models look better than they were. I fixed them and rebuilt the grader with 18 unit tests. Then I checked the AI judge against my own verdicts. 17 of its 42 earlier calls changed, so I threw its numbers out until it agreed with a person.
The decision that mattered most
I left every result I had to correct in the log next to the fixed one, so anyone reading it can see what went wrong and what changed.
What it found
On problems with one checkable answer, the group added nothing over a simple vote. On the open problem the cheap group found a flaw the strong model had missed, at about 1/70 of the cost, but the strong model was still better at actually building the solution. That was one trial and I was the judge, so I don't put much weight on it yet.

Source: council/FINDINGS.md (August 2026) · code and findings: github.com/calvinslinde/council

Blinx Co-op: a 2002 Xbox game, online for my friends and family

I got Blinx: The Time Sweeper running on Mac, Windows, and in the browser with up to six players online, so my friends and family could play it together. I built it with Claude. I decoded the first file on October 3, 2026 and friends had Mac and Windows releases by October 10.

What it took
I had to decode the game's file formats, rebuild it to run natively, and add online play that keeps every player's game identical. The releases update themselves.
The decision that mattered most
I don't ship any game files. Everyone uses their own copy of the game and everything else gets built from it on their machine.
What was hard
Keeping the Mac and Windows builds identical took two fixes. I had to match the original console's number rounding exactly, and I found two errors in the decompiled code. I checked 14 stages and got 0 desyncs.

Sources: blinx/STATUS.md · blinx/JOURNAL.md (October 2026). The release is private because the game is copyrighted.

How I check AI's work

  • I wrote a procedure that keeps asking why something is true and how we know, until every step ends in a fact, a definition, or "unknown." I revised it on September 10, 2026 after I caught it accepting a sourced fact inside an invalid inference.
  • I set up an agent that checks facts against the actual papers on PubMed and the primary sources, and reports a verdict and how sure it is.
  • I don't build on anything unconfirmed. If a fact can't be confirmed in its primary source, the work stops until it can.
  • I test the tester. The line I took from Council: "An unverified harness manufactures capability that isn't there."
